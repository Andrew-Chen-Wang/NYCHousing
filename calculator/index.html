Zoning & land use

  • Floor-area and height limitsCity · 1916; rewritten 1961

    Zoning caps how much floor area (FAR) and how tall a building may be on each lot, so land cost is spread over fewer homes.

    Upzone near transit: +25% floor area: $52K per home, −$335/month rent

  • Residential parking minimumsCity · 1961; narrowed 1982 (Manhattan Core) and 2024 (City of Yes)

    Zoning requires off-street parking for new apartments outside the transit core, at $30,000–$70,000+ a space.

    Doesn't apply here: Neighborhoods in parking Zones 2 and 3, and Lower Density Growth Management Areas. Zone 1 has no mandate.

  • Lower Density Growth Management Area rulesCity · 2004

    On Staten Island and in the east Bronx, extra parking (1.5 spaces per home in low-density districts), bigger yards and larger lots.

    Doesn't apply here: Staten Island and Bronx Community District 10 (Throgs Neck, Pelham Bay, City Island, Co-op City).

  • Setbacks, sky-exposure planes, yards and lot coverageCity · 1916 (setbacks); 1961 and 1987 (contextual envelopes)

    Rules that shape the building envelope force step-backs, transfer beams and awkward floor plates.

    Simplify to a single base height and rear yard: $13K per home, −$86/month rent

  • Bicycle parking, street trees and planting stripsCity · 2008 (street trees); 2009 (bicycle parking)

    Required bike rooms for 10+ units, street trees and planting strips with every new building.

    Kept. Removing it would save $1,108 per home and $7/month in rent.

Review & approvals

  • ULURP and Council member deferenceCity · 1975 (ULURP); 1989 (Council final vote)

    Any rezoning goes through a 7-month public review after years of pre-certification, and the local Council member effectively decides.

    Doesn't apply here: Only projects that need a rezoning or special permit (switch on 'Needs a rezoning').

  • Environmental review (CEQR / SEQRA)State · 1975 (SEQRA); 1977 (CEQR, Executive Order 91)

    Discretionary approvals require environmental review; since May 2026 most housing up to 250 units (500 in dense districts) is exempt.

    Doesn't apply here: Only projects needing a discretionary approval (rezoning); as-of-right projects skip it.

  • Landmarks Preservation Commission reviewCity · 1965

    In historic districts, new buildings need a Certificate of Appropriateness: hearings, redesigns and costlier materials.

    Doesn't apply here: Sites in historic districts (switch on 'In a historic district').

  • Building permits and multi-agency sign-offsCity · 1938 code onward; DOB NOW since 2016

    Plan review at Buildings plus separate approvals from FDNY, DEP (water, sewer, site connection), DOT (curb cuts, builders pavement plans) and others.

    Professional self-certification, parallel agency review and a 30-day shot clock: $11K per home, −$69/month rent, 3.0 months faster

  • Utility service connections (Con Edison)Utility · Public Service Law; Con Edison tariffs

    New service needs Con Edison design, equipment and street work on the utility's schedule; delays can hold a finished building empty.

    PSC-ordered connection deadlines with penalties: $5,233 per home, −$34/month rent, 1.8 months faster

  • E-designations and environmental remediationCity · 1980s (E-designations); 2008 (Office of Environmental Remediation)

    Lots flagged during rezonings for hazardous materials, noise or air quality need Office of Environmental Remediation sign-off before permits.

    Doesn't apply here: Rezoned sites, where E-designations are usually placed.

Building code & design

  • Two-stair requirement for mid-rise apartmentsCity · 1968 code; current NYC Building Code §1006

    Apartment buildings above small floor plates need two exit stairs, which forces double-loaded corridors and fewer, smaller family units.

    Allow one sprinklered stair up to six stories: $27K per home, −$171/month rent

  • Limits on wood-frame and mass-timber constructionCity · Fire limits since the 19th century; 2022 code (mass timber to 85 ft)

    The code effectively requires concrete or steel for most apartment buildings and caps mass timber at 85 feet.

    Allow sprinklered light-frame to five stories and mass timber to IBC heights: $22K per home, −$142/month rent

  • Metal plumbing pipe requirementsCity · Long-standing; 2004 state veto; NYC Plumbing Code §605

    The plumbing code requires metal piping in most taller buildings and limits PEX and CPVC.

    Adopt the International Plumbing Code's material list: $5,542 per home, −$36/month rent

  • Metal conduit and armored cable requirementsCity · NYC Electrical Code (amended NEC)

    The electrical code requires specific metal conduits and armored cable where most states allow plastic-sheathed cable.

    Allow NM cable inside units of sprinklered buildings: $3,879 per home, −$25/month rent

  • Accessibility beyond federal lawCity · 1987 (Local Law 58); 2008 and 2014 codes

    New York's code makes every unit in new multifamily buildings accessible or adaptable, beyond the federal Fair Housing Act.

    Kept. Removing it would save $3,695 per home and $24/month in rent.

  • Sprinklers, fire alarms and high-rise fire systemsCity · 1999 (residential sprinklers, Local Law 10); 2008 code

    Sprinklers, standpipes, alarms and, in tall buildings, fire command centers and pressurized stairs.

    Kept. Removing it would save $9,237 per home and $59/month in rent.

  • Light, air and minimum room-size rulesState · 1901 (Tenement House Act); 1929 (Multiple Dwelling Law)

    The Multiple Dwelling Law and Housing Maintenance Code set window, court and room-size minimums that shape every floor plan.

    Modern performance standards for light and ventilation: $7,907 per home, −$51/month rent

Energy & environment

  • NYC Energy Conservation Code (stretch code)City · 2010; updated every three years

    Insulation, window and mechanical efficiency standards stricter than the national model code.

    Kept. Removing it would save $7,389 per home and $47/month in rent.

  • All-electric new buildings (Local Law 154)City · 2021

    New buildings cannot burn gas or oil: heat pumps for heat and hot water, induction cooking.

    Kept. Removing it would save $1,847 per home and $12/month in rent.

  • Building emissions caps (Local Law 97)City · 2019 (Climate Mobilization Act)

    Buildings over 25,000 square feet face annual carbon caps and fines.

    Kept. Removing it would save $0 per home and $6/month in rent.

  • Solar or green roof mandate (Local Laws 92 and 94)City · 2019

    New buildings must cover available roof area with solar panels or a green roof.

    Solar-ready roof or a payment in lieu: $1,108 per home, −$7/month rent

  • Bird-friendly glass (Local Law 15)City · 2020 (effective January 2021)

    Patterned or treated glass on 90% of the facade up to 75 feet.

    Exempt affordable and mostly-masonry residential buildings: $739 per home, −$5/month rent

  • Unified Stormwater RuleCity · 2022

    Sites disturbing 20,000 square feet or adding 5,000 of impervious surface must detain and retain stormwater.

    Doesn't apply here: Larger sites (our high-rise prototype).

  • Flood-resistant construction (Appendix G)City · 1983; strengthened 2013 after Hurricane Sandy

    In the floodplain, living space must sit above the design flood elevation, with flood-proofed lower floors.

    Doesn't apply here: Sites in the 1% annual-chance floodplain (switch on 'In the floodplain').

Construction site, labor & insurance

  • Scaffold Law (absolute liability for falls)State · 1885

    Owners and contractors are fully liable for any gravity-related worker injury, even when the worker was partly at fault. New York is the only state left with this rule.

    Replace absolute liability with comparative negligence, as in every other state: $19K per home, −$124/month rent

  • 485-x construction wage floorsState · 2024

    Projects of 100+ units that use the 485-x tax exemption must pay at least $40/hour; 150+ units in Manhattan below 96th St and parts of Brooklyn and Queens pay up to $76/hour.

    Doesn't apply here: Projects of 100+ units using the 485-x exemption.

  • Site safety training (Local Law 196)City · 2017

    Every worker needs 40 hours of safety training and supervisors 62 hours before working on most sites.

    Kept. Removing it would save $2,217 per home and $14/month in rent.

  • Site safety managers and crane operator licensingCity · Site safety: 1990s, expanded 2008; crane licenses: Title 28 Article 405, Local Law 80 of 2017

    Major buildings need a full-time licensed site safety manager, and New York licenses its own crane operators beyond national certification.

    Doesn't apply here: High-rise buildings.

  • Work-hour limits, sidewalk sheds and construction fencesCity · Noise Code 2007; shed rules 1980 onward; Local Laws 47–48 of 2025

    Work is limited to weekdays 7am–6pm without after-hours variances, and sheds and fences are required along the sidewalk.

    As-of-right extended hours for interior work: $881 per home, −$6/month rent, 0.3 months faster

Taxes & fees

  • Higher property taxes on rental buildings (Class 2)State · 1981 (classified property tax, S.7000-A)

    Rental buildings pay an effective tax rate about twice that of one- and two-family homes, roughly 30% of a new building's income.

    Doesn't apply here: Fully taxed projects (choose 'Full property tax' in the calculator).

  • Mortgage recording tax on construction loansState · 1906 (state); city rates added later

    A 2.8% tax on the full amount of commercial mortgages over $500,000, including construction loans.

    Exempt construction loans for new housing: $11K per home, −$68/month rent

  • Real property transfer taxes on landCity · 1959 (city RPTT); state since 1968

    City (2.625%) and state (0.65%) transfer taxes on commercial land sales over $2 million.

    Kept. Removing it would save $8,077 per home and $52/month in rent.

  • Sales tax on construction materialsState · 1934 (city sales tax); 1965 (state)

    8.875% combined sales tax on materials used in new buildings; exempt only for exempt owners.

    Kept. Removing it would save $14K per home and $92/month in rent.

  • Permit, filing and connection feesCity · Various

    DOB filing and permit fees, DEP water and sewer connection charges, DOT street permits.

    Kept. Removing it would save $1,406 per home and $9/month in rent.

Affordability & tenancy

  • Mandatory Inclusionary HousingCity · 2016

    In MIH areas, 20–30% of homes must be permanently affordable, typically 25% at an average of 60% of area median income.

    Doesn't apply here: Rezoned sites and neighborhoods with MIH mapped, buildings over 10 units.

  • 485-x affordability set-asideState · 2024

    To get the 485-x tax exemption, 20–25% of homes must be rent-stabilized at an average of 80% of area median income.

    Kept. Removing it would save $0 per home and $452/month in rent.

  • Affordable unit marketing and Housing Connect lotteryCity · 1980s marketing guidelines; Housing Connect 2013

    Income-restricted units must be leased through HPD's lottery, with income verification and long lease-up.

    Pre-qualified applicant pools and 60-day lease-up targets: $564 per home, −$4/month rent

Federal & materials

  • Federal tariffs on steel, aluminum, copper and lumberFederal · 2018; raised 2025

    Steel and aluminum tariffs rose to 50% in June 2025, with tariffs on copper products and Canadian lumber.

    Repeal construction-material tariffs: $9,237 per home, −$59/month rent
