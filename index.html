What it costs to build a home in New York, and which rules could go

New York is the most expensive place in the world to build [Turner & Townsend 2025; NRCA / Turner & Townsend 2026]. This site lays out every cost in a typical apartment project, from land to the mortgage recording tax, and 46 laws and rules that add to them, each with its history and citations. Then a calculator lets you choose which to keep and see the change in total development cost and in the rent a new home has to charge.

Why so few projects pencil
A new market-rate home here must rent for about $4,859 a month to earn the 5.5% return lenders and investors require, while a new building in Bedford-Stuyvesant can charge roughly $4,648. Across the city this building pencils in 16 of 170 neighborhoods under current law and 160 with the suggested changes. That gap is why the city's vacancy rate is 1.4%, the lowest since 1968 [Vital City 2025].

Suggested starting point

Not everything should go. Of 46 rules, the calculator starts with 9 removed, 18 reformed and 19 kept. Removal is suggested where evidence says the rule adds cost without matching benefit (the Scaffold Law's absolute liability, the two-stair rule, parking mandates); reform where the goal is right but the method is costly (ULURP's member deference, landmarks review of new buildings); and keep where the protection is worth it (sprinklers, flood-resistant construction, accessibility, all-electric buildings).

  • Lower Density Growth Management Area rules

  • 485-x construction wage floors

  • Federal tariffs on steel, aluminum, copper and lumber

  • Prevailing wage on subsidized and public-land housing
